> Part One started from one person, one plot of land and one year's harvest and derived today's financial institutions. Part Two changed one parameter and showed which constraints loosen, which tighten and which stay the same. Part Three turns in another direction: instead of deriving a whole civilization, it derives a product. The starting point is one person and one sum of money, and each chapter adds just one user-side constraint: he has his own goals and capacity to bear losses; he cannot be present at all times; he cannot see what the agent is doing; the agent makes mistakes; trust takes time. Each constraint makes the product grow a part. Together, the five parts make a financial product that can stand in a world where cognitive labour is almost free.

## 1. Deriving in another direction

The first two parts asked "what institutions does this world need"; this part asks "what product does this person need". The method is unchanged: first ask what happens without it, then see what grows out of that. The part that grows solves the old problem and creates a new one, which becomes the constraint of the next step. The principles obtained in the first two parts become the basis of product design here: each new part must state which principle it rests on and which unchanging constraint limits it.

## 3. Summary of Part Three

Starting from one person and one sum of money, five user-side constraints grow five parts of a product. First, state clearly what is promised: promise the process, not the result. Next, write down the agreement and tiered authority, so that the agent knows what to do when the user is not present. Next, keep records that a third party holds, layered and verifiable, so that the user can see the process. Next, set up defences in layers, slow down where things move fast, and write down in advance how compensation works for each kind of error. Finally, start small, borrow costly signals, widen authority step by step on the record of kept agreements, and after an error take one step back rather than returning to zero.

The five parts do not stand alone. The promise determines what the agreement says; the agreement determines what the records check; the records determine which kind an error belongs to and who pays for it; compensation and limits determine how quickly trust can widen. Without any one part, the others cannot stand.
